Sealant guns, in full

A caulk gun is judged on three days: the day the trigger gets sloppy, the day it starts dripping, and the day the rod bends. TILER STAR guns are built to be taken apart instead of thrown away — every one comes apart with hand tools, so a worn part is a part you replace, not a gun you replace.

Why the ratio box is empty. US buyers shop this category on thrust ratio — 12:1, 18:1, 26:1 — which is force multiplication at the trigger, a dimensionless number. 992 lbf is an absolute output force. They are different measurements and one is not the other, so we publish the one we have and leave the one we don’t.

04.1 — Snap-off blade steel

AISI 1080OIL-QUENCHED CARBON STEEL · 0.75–0.88 % C

A knife body lasts years. The edge lasts a shift. These blades are high-carbon steel quenched in oil for a hard, fine edge that survives abrasive board instead of rolling on it. The geometry below is not a marketing figure — it is printed on the dispenser in your hand, and we read it off the box.

Hardness in HRC is deliberately absent. AISI 1080 has a published material range; we have no tested value for these blades, and a material range is not a test result.

04.2 — One-piece stainless spatulas

No joint. Nothing to loosen.

A putty knife fails in one place: where the blade meets the handle. Rivets loosen. Ferrules pack with thinset. Wood swells. Plastic snaps the first time you use it as a pry bar — and you will use it as a pry bar. Every spatula here is cut from a single piece of stainless, blade through handle, so there is nothing to work loose and nothing to trap material.
