The system

A kit for one trade,
not a wall of SKUs.

Twenty-seven parts is not a warehouse. It is the set of things a tile setter actually carries — and every one of them has a job in the same sequence, in the same order you do the work.

About

We build tools for
the last two inches.

We make seven things and we make them without compromise: snap-off knives, replacement blades, one-piece stainless spatulas, skeleton caulk guns, a 600 ml sausage gun with the nozzles that feed it, work gloves, and a magnetic tool holder. There is no cordless line, no accessory wall, no seasonal collection.

Our approach is simple. We remove the failure points. Spatulas are cut from a single piece of stainless — no ferrule, no rivet, no handle to work loose in a bucket of thinset. Blades are AISI 1080 carbon steel, quenched in oil so the edge holds through a full board of backer. Caulk guns come apart with hand tools, because a tool you can rebuild is a tool you keep.

And we tell you where every tool is made. Our supply chain runs across three countries, because we buy each part where it is made best — and because the buyer deserves to know that before he pays, not after. Where a factory has not yet certified an origin in writing, this site says so plainly rather than filling the gap with a flag.

Sizes 7 to 11

A glove that starts where most ranges stop.

Most trade glove ranges effectively begin at size 8. Ours begins at a true 7, measured in inches around the palm, so the person with smaller hands buys a work glove instead of a compromise.

Wrap a tape around your dominant hand at the widest part of the palm, just below the knuckles, thumb left out. The number in inches is your size. Between sizes? Go down — a work glove should be snug, not loose.
